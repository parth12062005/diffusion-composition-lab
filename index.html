Why does the board example need no annealing?

Its opposite means cancel and its expert variances stay at 1. The current product stays Normal(0, 0.5), so its score stays -2*x at every t. Langevin can sample that fixed target. Try Unequal widths to see a changing intermediate target.

At one fixed t

The score attracts particles. Fresh noise spreads them. Their balance samples a distribution rather than finding only its peak.

Across noise levels

Annealed sampling runs several updates, lowers t, then repeats. This demo isolates one level so you can inspect the balance.

A numerical illustration of unadjusted Langevin sampling, not a neural network training run. Finite step size adds bias. Paper: Section 4.1 and Algorithm 1

03 / Our image experiment

MNIST: AND and NOT

We train the experts separately, freeze them, then compare samplers. Results are pending.

Expert A learns

1 6

A AND B

q = pA*pB/Z

score = sA + sB

Hypothesis: favour the shared digit 1.

A AND NOT B

q = (pA/pB^alpha)/Z

score = sA - alpha*sB

Hypothesis: suppress 1 and favour 0.

These are soft density operations. A ratio can amplify unrealistic regions where B is tiny. A normalized NOT target needs a finite integral. The Gaussian lab shows when that condition fails.

04 / What we will test

The same experts.
A different sampling method.

A fair comparison

Keep networks fixed. Match expert evaluations. Record runtime, initialization and seed variation. Include A alone as the alpha = 0 control.

What counts as evidence

Use the 1D case for exact checks. Use real MNIST grids for quality and class behaviour. Report failures alongside improvements.

What else does the paper offer?

Energy models allow adjusted samplers such as MALA. The paper also studies mixtures, guidance and spatial composition. These extensions are outside this experiment. A score-only network does not directly provide the log-density differences required by MALA.
